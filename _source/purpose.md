The purpose of the Primary Mental Health Care Minimum Data Set (PMHC MDS) is to
provide comparable, meaningful national and regional data that supports safe, equitable and
nationally consistent delivery of primary mental health care. The PMHC MDS aims to
strengthen quality oversight, inform service planning, delivery, evaluation and policy
development, support effective system stewardship, and drive improved mental health
consumer-centred outcomes. Its purpose is to meet the following objectives.

## <a id="community_objectives"></a>Community objectives

At the community level, the PMHC MDS aims to:
* Provide a robust evidence base for Primary Health Networks (PHNs) to monitor
  service delivery by commissioned organisations across key performance domains,
  inform judgements about mental health consumer - centred outcomes and value for
  money, and support effective local program evaluation.
* Enable community needs analysis and planning by contributing data that supports the
  identification of service coverage, gaps, priorities and inequities. This includes access
  for rural and regional communities and priority populations such as First Nations
  peoples, lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, intersex, queer, asexual and other
  diverse sexual orientations, sex characteristics and gender identities, culturally and
  racially marginalised groups, younger and older people, and people with high support
  and/or co-occurring needs.
* Provide meaningful data for benchmarking both within and across regions to support
  improvement in service quality and mental health consumer outcomes at the local
  level.
* Inform communication with community-level stakeholders, including the broader
  community, through appropriate reporting based on information that is comparable to
  other regions.

## <a id="national_objectives"></a>National objectives

At the national and state/territory levels, the PMHC MDS aims to:
* Provide the basis for national quality oversight and ongoing improvement in the way
  primary mental health care models and reforms are being implemented, and whether
  they meet core requirements expected.
* Serve as a foundation for accountability arrangements with PHNs, including shared
  accountability with state and territory mental health services, and inform regular
  review and updating of annual activity work plans.
* Provide an evidence base for evaluating mental health reforms and supporting
  broader research and evaluation across mental health and other sectors, including
  enabling future data integration where appropriate, to drive improved outcomes for
  mental health consumers
* Enable ongoing national planning for primary mental health care by providing data to
  inform demand forecasting, workforce planning, including the lived experience peer
  workforce, and evidence-informed policy development.
* Support appropriate public reporting that informs mental health consumers and the
  Australian community about service activity and outcomes achieved.
